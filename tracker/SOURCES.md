# Where the advice comes from

Three sources shape the guidance in this tracker. Each is credited on the
**Stack → Sources** page in the app, alongside the places they contradict one
another.

## Andrew Huberman — *Huberman Lab*

Stanford neuroscientist; podcast and published protocols.

**Used for:** the daily protocol on Today, the sleep toolkit, and the
wider-week page in Train.

- **Morning sunlight** within 30–60 minutes of waking: about 10 minutes on a
  clear morning, 20–30 if overcast, outdoors — glass filters most of what sets
  the circadian clock. He treats this as the highest-leverage single item.
- **Delay caffeine 90–120 minutes** after waking. Cortisol peaks 30–45 minutes
  in and holds for roughly 90; caffeine inside that window competes with your own
  alertness and buys a mid-morning crash instead.
- **NSDR** (non-sleep deep rest), 10–30 minutes in the early afternoon, in place
  of a second coffee.
- **Sleep toolkit, in priority order:** light → temperature → caffeine →
  supplements. Bedroom around 18–19 °C; a warm shower 60–90 minutes before bed so
  core temperature falls as you get in; sleep requires a drop of 1–3 °C. He is
  explicit that most people who fix the first three never need the fourth.
- **Foundational fitness:** three or more resistance sessions a week, alternating
  strength (4–8 reps) and hypertrophy (8–15) blocks roughly monthly; 150–200
  minutes of zone 2 cardio a week; one long and one short high-intensity effort;
  heat or cold for recovery.

Base 12 already exceeds the resistance-training half. The gap is aerobic, which
is why zone-2 minutes are now tracked and the rest days carry the load.

## Brian Sanders — *Food Lies* / Peak Human

Filmmaker behind the *Food Lies* documentary; host of the Peak Human podcast;
author of the SAPIEN framework.

**Used for:** the Food quality page, the quality tier on every logged meal, and
the nutrient-dense additions to the meal library.

The framework, in his terms:

1. Nutrient-dense, highly bioavailable whole foods
2. Focus on protein, embrace fat, minimise carbohydrate
3. Avoid sugar, refined grains and vegetable oils
4. Don't always be eating; eat with the seasons
5. Reduce toxicity in food choices

Points 1, 3, 4 and 5 are implemented directly. Point 2 is the genuine conflict —
see below.

## Brendan Ruh — *Santa Cruz Medicinals*

Founder (2018); UC Santa Cruz medical anthropology; Santa Cruz Paleo.

**Used for:** how the Stack pages judge a supplement, and the ultra-processed
flag on the food log.

- An **effective dose or nothing** — most products carry a fraction of the dose
  the study used, which makes them a cheaper way to buy nothing.
- A **third-party certificate of analysis** for purity and potency.
- **No proprietary blends** — a blend hides the doses inside it.
- **Nothing in there for the manufacturer's convenience** — fillers, unnecessary
  flavourings, gelatin shells.
- **Price per effective dose**, not per tub.

His public position on ultra-processed food is unambiguous. On seed oils he is
more measured than the internet version of the argument: worth avoiding, not
worth fearing in moderation.

## Where they disagree

**Carbohydrate.** Food Lies argues for minimising it. This programme is five hard
sessions a week in a surplus, and heavy resistance training runs on muscle
glycogen. Both can be true — the Targets page carries a **carbohydrate approach**
switch with three settings, and the honest trade is that a low-carbohydrate lean
gain usually costs some training volume for the first few weeks while you adapt.

| Setting | Fat | Carbohydrate |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | 0.9 g/kg | whatever energy is left (~455 g) |
| Moderate | 1.3 g/kg | the remainder (~370 g) |
| Lower-carb (Sapien) | the remainder | capped at 25% of energy (~190 g) |

**Seed oils.** The strong, uncontested version of the claim is that the foods
carrying most of them are ultra-processed anyway — which is why the tracker
flags processing tier rather than counting oil grams.

**Supplements.** Huberman discusses a much longer list than the other two, and
some of it rests on thin human evidence. Tongkat Ali and Fadogia agrestis are
named on the **Skip** page with the reason rather than quietly omitted — Fadogia
in particular has essentially no human safety data and rodent studies showing
testicular toxicity at higher doses.

**None of them is your doctor.** A neuroscientist, a filmmaker and a supplement
founder. Where the advice is cheap and reversible — light, sleep timing, walking,
cooking your own food — it costs nothing to try. Be slower with anything you
swallow.
