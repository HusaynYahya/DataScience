# Diet and supplements — lean muscle gain, halal

Worked for a 21-year-old man, 178 cm, 75 kg, training five days a week. Substitute
your own figures; the tracker recalculates everything from the same formulas.

## How the targets are derived

**Resting metabolic rate** — Mifflin–St Jeor, the most accurate of the common
equations for people who aren't very lean or very heavy:

```
RMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
    = 10 × 75 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 21 + 5 = 1,763 kcal
```

**Maintenance** — RMR × activity factor. Four to five hard sessions a week plus
ordinary daily movement is 1.55:

```
1,763 × 1.55 ≈ 2,730 kcal
```

**Lean-gain target** — maintenance + about 11%, which is roughly +300 kcal. Enough
surplus to build, small enough that most of the gain is muscle:

```
≈ 3,030 kcal/day
```

| | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 3,030 kcal | Maintenance +11% |
| Protein | 150 g | 2.0 g/kg — the top of where the evidence still shows a return |
| Fat | 68 g | 0.9 g/kg — above the hormonal floor, not eating into carbohydrate |
| Carbohydrate | 455 g | Whatever energy is left. This is the training fuel. |
| Fibre | 42 g | 14 g per 1,000 kcal |
| Saturated fat | ≤ 34 g | 10% of energy |
| Water | 3.1 L | 35 ml/kg plus 500 ml for training |

Micronutrient targets are the US RDA for men aged 19–30: calcium 1,000 mg, iron
8 mg, potassium 3,400 mg, magnesium 400 mg, zinc 11 mg, vitamin C 90 mg, vitamin D
15 µg, vitamin A 900 µg, B12 2.4 µg, folate 400 µg. Sodium is a ceiling at
2,300 mg, and EPA+DHA is set at 500 mg.

## Six rules that do the work

1. **Protein first, every meal.** Four feeds of 35–45 g beats two of 75 g. Build
   the plate around the protein and let carbohydrate fill the rest.
2. **Carbohydrate is the training input.** On Monday, Tuesday and Thursday, put
   the biggest carbohydrate meal within three hours either side of the session.
3. **Fat has a floor, not a target.** Below about 0.7 g/kg you pay for it in
   hormones and satiety. Above that it is just energy, and carbohydrate does more
   for you in a lean gain.
4. **Weigh yourself the same way** — every morning, after the toilet, before food.
   Use the seven-day average. A salty dinner can move the scale 1.5 kg overnight.
5. **Adjust fortnightly, not daily.** Two weeks of a flat average means add
   150 kcal. Gaining faster than 0.5% of bodyweight a week means take 150 off.
6. **Fibre and fluid do the quiet work.** Forty grams of fibre and three litres of
   water are what make 3,000 kcal a day survivable.

## Supplements

### Core — three things

| | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 5 g daily | The best-evidenced supplement in strength sport: roughly 5–15% more reps at a given load over a block. No loading phase — 5 g a day saturates you in about three weeks. Take it every day, rest days included, at any time. |
| **Vitamin D3** | 1,000–2,000 IU with a meal | At UK latitude your skin makes essentially none from October to April. Deficiency drags on strength, mood and immunity. Take it with something fatty. |
| **Whey or casein** | 25–30 g, as needed | Convenient protein, nothing more. Use it only to close the gap between what you ate and 150 g. Casein before bed if the evening is thin. |

### Situational — only when the condition holds

- **Omega-3 (EPA+DHA), 1 g** — if you eat oily fish fewer than twice a week. Check the label for actual EPA/DHA, not total fish oil.
- **Caffeine, 3 mg/kg (≈225 mg) 45 min pre-session** — reliably improves output. Nothing after 2 pm; the half-life is about five hours and sleep is where training becomes muscle.
- **Magnesium glycinate, 200–300 mg in the evening** — only if sleep is poor or the diet is short on greens, nuts and legumes. Glycinate, not oxide.
- **Electrolytes** — long sessions, hot rooms, fasting days. Sodium and potassium, not a sugar drink in disguise.
- **Multivitamin** — cheap insurance against a bad week. Not a substitute for vegetables.

### Skip these

- **BCAAs and EAAs** — redundant once you eat 1.6 g/kg of protein.
- **Testosterone boosters** — at 21 yours is as high as it will ever be. Sleep, bodyweight and training move it; nothing on a shelf does.
- **Fat burners** — caffeine and stimulants at an undisclosed dose, at a markup.
- **Mass gainers** — sugar and maltodextrin at four times the price of oats, milk and peanut butter.
- **Glutamine** — well studied, consistently does nothing for trained people eating enough protein.
- **Proprietary-blend pre-workouts** — if the label hides the doses, you can't know anything in it is dosed to work.

### Halal check

Two things to check on any capsule: the **shell** (bovine or porcine gelatin is
common — look for vegetarian/HPMC capsules, or buy powders) and **flavourings**,
which occasionally carry alcohol as a solvent. Creatine monohydrate, whey isolate
and D3 all come in powder or veg-cap form. Vitamin D3 is usually lanolin-derived,
which is fine; lichen-derived D3 is the vegan option.
