# Base 12 — a twelve-week training block

Five sessions a week, three heavy and two skill-led, for a 21-year-old man with a
full gym and access to a pull-up bar and rings. Barbell work carries the load;
calisthenics carries the skill. Drop Saturday for a four-day week — nothing else
changes.

| Day | Session | Focus | Working sets |
|-----|---------|-------|--------------|
| Mon | Push | Chest · shoulders · triceps | 22 |
| Tue | Pull | Back · rear delts · biceps | 23 |
| Wed | Rest | — | — |
| Thu | Legs & core | Quads · hamstrings · trunk | 23 |
| Fri | Skill & upper volume | Handstand · lever · muscle-up | 21 |
| Sat | Full body & carries | Posterior chain · grip · engine | 20 |
| Sun | Rest | — | — |

That lands every muscle group between 12 and 20 hard sets a week, which is where
the hypertrophy evidence sits, and it gives the pressing and pulling patterns two
exposures each.

## The progression rule

**Double progression**, on everything. Each exercise has a rep range. When every
working set reaches the top of that range at RPE 8 or lower, add weight next
session:

- upper body: +2.5 kg (+1.25 kg on the overhead press)
- lower body: +5 kg
- weighted pull-ups and dips: +1.25–2.5 kg

Then work back up the range from the bottom. Skills progress by hold time and
clean reps, never by adding load.

RPE 8 means two good reps left in the tank. If a set turns into a grind, the set
above it was already too heavy.

## Warm-up — eight minutes, every session

1. Easy cardio, 2 minutes, bike or rower
2. Band pull-apart × 20
3. Scapular pull-up × 10
4. Dead bug × 10 each side
5. Wrist prep, 30 s each direction (mandatory on Friday)
6. Ramp-up sets on lift one: 50%, 70%, 85% of the working weight

## Skill ladders

Move up a rung when you own the current one — 3 × 15 s with straight arms for
holds, or clean reps at the top of the range for the rest.

**Front lever** → tuck (15 s) → advanced tuck → one leg extended → straddle → **full lever, 8 s**

**Muscle-up** → pull-up to sternum → explosive pull to lower ribs → transition negative → kipping → **strict muscle-up**
*Roughly a bodyweight-plus-25% pull-up makes the strict version turn up on its own.*

**Handstand** → wall plank 60 s → chest-to-wall 60 s → kick-up to balance → freestanding 10 s → **freestanding 30 s**

**Handstand push-up** → pike push-up → feet-elevated pike → deficit pike on parallettes → wall HSPU partial → **wall HSPU full range**
*Gated by the overhead press — around 0.75 × bodyweight makes it straightforward.*

**Pistol squat** → box squat to a bench → assisted → heel-elevated → full pistol → **weighted pistol**
*Ankle mobility usually limits this before strength does.*

## The twelve weeks

| Weeks | Phase | What changes |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| 1–3 | Wave one — build | Learn the lifts, set the baseline. RPE 7 in week 1, RPE 8 by week 3. |
| 4 | Deload | One set fewer everywhere, 10% off the bar. Skills technical only. |
| 5–7 | Wave two — load | Restart 2.5% above wave one's best, push to RPE 8–9 on top sets. |
| 8 | Deload | As week 4. Film the handstand and lever — technique drifts unnoticed. |
| 9–11 | Wave three — express | Heaviest wave. RPE 9 on first sets in week 11. Attempt two new ladder rungs. |
| 12 | Test & reset | 3RM bench, squat, trap bar; max weighted pull-up; longest lever and handstand holds. Then five easy days and start again from the new numbers. |

## What to expect

At 21, eating in a small surplus, sleeping well and running this honestly:
0.25–0.5% of bodyweight added per week, most of it in the first two waves. Squat
and bench should move 10–20 kg across the block; weighted pull-ups typically more,
proportionally, because most people start them undertrained.
